# Writing rules

These rules apply to everything the candidate will send or publish: resume text, cover
letters, application-form answers. They do not apply to evaluation notes, which are for the
candidate's eyes and should simply be direct.

## Voice

- Plain and specific. Name the tool, the system, the number, the customer when the sources
  allow it. "Postgres with pgvector over 12k documents" beats "a scalable retrieval
  architecture".
- Short sentences, mostly active voice. Vary length so it reads like a person: a short one,
  then one with context, then short again.
- Confident, not boastful. State what was done and what it produced; let the reader judge.
- No hedging filler ("I believe I could possibly"), no exclamation marks, no rhetorical
  questions.
- Match the register of the document: formal and keyword-dense in the resume; natural
  first-person prose in letters and answers.
- The PROFILE may carry a `writing.tone` preference and the NOTES may describe how the
  candidate likes to sound. Follow them when present; they override the defaults here on
  tone, never on facts.
- Write in the language of the posting or question. Default to English.

## Banned phrases

Do not use these or their close relatives; replace each with the concrete thing it hides:

- passionate about, results-oriented, results-driven, proven track record, self-starter,
  team player, go-getter, detail-oriented
- leveraged (say "used", or name the tool), spearheaded (say "led" or "ran"), facilitated
  (say "ran" or "set up"), utilised (say "used")
- synergy, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, innovative, state-of-the-art, best-in-class,
  world-class, dynamic, impactful
- in today's fast-paced world, demonstrated ability to, best practices (name the practice),
  responsible for (say what was done), thinking outside the box, hit the ground running
- "I am writing to express my interest", "I believe I would be a great fit", "please do not
  hesitate to contact me", "I look forward to hearing from you"

Also avoid: em dashes, smart quotes, emoji, and lists of three adjectives.

## Resume register

- No first person, no pronouns at all: every line starts with a verb or a noun.
- Past tense for past roles, present tense for the current one, consistently.
- Keywords in normal casing inside sentences, not Title-Cased lists.
- No adjectives about character; only facts about work.

## Cover letters

A cover letter answers one question: why this person for this role, now. 220 to 320 words,
plain text, no headings, no bullet lists, no placeholders.

1. Open with the role and the single sharpest reason the candidate fits it. No preamble.
2. Two short paragraphs that take the posting's two or three most important requirements
   and answer each with a concrete CV fact: what was built, for whom, with what result.
   Use the posting's own words for the requirement so the reader sees the mapping.
3. If relocation, remote work or sponsorship is relevant to this role, one plain sentence
   stating the candidate's situation as the PROFILE describes it, framed positively ("already
   authorised to work in the Netherlands", "available to relocate to Berlin").
4. Close in one line that says what the candidate wants next, and sign with the full name
   from the PROFILE.

First person is right here. Facts only from the sources; the tone may be warm, the claims
may not be new. Never address a company by a name other than the one in the posting.

## Form answers

- Lead with the answer; explanations come after.
- The reader is a recruiter resolving a specific doubt (motivation, stack fit, seniority,
  logistics). Work out which doubt the question is really about and answer that.
- Reference something specific to this posting in every free-text answer, so it cannot be
  mistaken for a template.
- Keep the candidate's voice: first person, contractions are fine, no corporate tone.
